# Principles

These rules outrank local taste. They match the public site as it is built: work on the home page, proof on the project and the post, contact when the visitor is ready.

## Work before biography

Lead with what was built or written. The about page and the resume hold the career story. The home page opens on the practice and the outcomes, then offers a way in.

Pass: the home hero names the work and links into the portfolio or contact.

Fail: a home page that opens on a settings panel, an analytics chart, or a newsletter popup.

## Proof before claims

A number or a superlative needs a place the visitor can open. "Approximately 60%" belongs next to the outcome it describes. A project claim belongs on that project's page, with the repository or the live link when one exists.

Pass: a project card title opens the project. The detail page shows the stack and the links.

Fail: a badge that says "enterprise grade" with no project behind it, or "trusted by" with no names.

Do not write "legally compliant", "live", or "AI-powered" as decoration. Name the thing: authentication, the pipeline, the post.

## One path before extra chrome

The visitor's next step is visible without a hunt.

- Home, portfolio, and a project page answer "what does he build?"
- Blog and a post answer "what has he written?"
- Contact answers "how do I reach him?"
- Resume and about answer "who is he?"
- Admin answers "what do I need to handle?"

Show the smallest set that supports the job. Related posts come after the article. Filters sit above the list they change. Column pickers and saved views do not belong on the first screen.

## The owner is not the visitor

Public pages never assume a session. Admin pages never pretend to be the portfolio. A reply, a publish, a purge, and a testimonial invite stay in admin.

Analytics on the public site respects the consent banner. Do not add a tracker that fires before consent.
